# IPDAMS v0.2.243 — immediate internal property transfer; policy checkpoint (2026-11-01)

Owner confirms all current property records are **test data**, and requests a simpler documentary workflow.

## Agreed behavior
- **INTERNAL (same school) transfer:** recording the ITR/PTR immediately updates the named receiving employee's property custodian in the same database transaction that creates the versioned document, movement, links, numbering and audit. No later signed-paper receipt step, employee login, extra confirmation, or Division Supply/Property Officer approval.
- **Paper documents:** printable ITR/PTR and companion RIS remain documentation; physically signed copies stay outside IPDAMS for any later verification. IPDAMS does not claim wet/electronic signatures or paper verification.
- **EXTERNAL (inter-school):** remains document-only pending; avoid cross-school asset organization reassignment until item catalog and IAR lineage protections are implemented. Do not silently move property across schools.
- **Previously pending internal ITR/PTR:** provide one-time “Apply saved internal transfer” action to reconcile without pretending a signature, rather than automatically changing historical records at installation. Previously RECEIVED and CANCELLED records remain untouched. Use a distinct `RECORDED` status for documentary internal custody updates.
- **Division Supply/Property Officer authorization (owner final workflow clarification, 2026-11-02):** For a **high-value semi-expendable item** (cost over PHP 5,000 and under PHP 50,000), there are **TWO distinct approval decisions**: (1) division approval before a proposed school declaration of **UNSERVICEABLE** becomes final; the asset stays in the **school property record** and its condition history, asset identity, and accountability are preserved; (2) when the item is actually proposed for **disposal**, a NEW, separate division approval authorizing the disposal transaction is required. An earlier unserviceable-approval ID must NOT satisfy or bypass disposal approval. The disposal remains PENDING until separately approved; approval alone does not remove the asset from school inventory. Only after the approved disposal is **physically completed and documented**, IPDAMS ends active asset and custodian accountability where authorized, excludes the item from active property and active ICS views, and keeps the historical asset, approvals, evidence, IIRUSP and disposal record in an auditable **DISPOSED** archive (never hard-delete). The school must keep the item visible as unserviceable/pending disposal between approvals. Ordinary condition updates and low-value unserviceable declarations do not require the extra Division approval under the owner policy, while statutory inspections, disposal committee/authorized disposal procedures and recordkeeping still apply. A request to delete/void an erroneously registered item is a **separate data correction** needing division approval and audit; not a substitute for physical disposal. Ordinary internal employee transfers remain immediate. Low/high threshold follows COA Circular 2022-004; PPE at PHP 50,000 or more follows a separate workflow. This specification is future work, not installed in v0.2.243.
- **ICS three-year renewal (owner clarification, 2026-11-02):** COA Circular 2022-004 Section 4.1 specifies that ICS is renewed **every three years, or upon change of custodian**, for all semi-expendable property (< PHP 50,000), regardless of low/high value. This **document renewal deadline is not the asset's estimated useful-life expiry or automatic extinguishment of accountability**. On anniversary, display **Renewal Due** and offer an authorized **Renew ICS** action; preserve original finalized ICS, create separately numbered renewal with a link to the preceding one, maintain custodian/asset identity, no automatic transfer, condition change or disposal. Do not mark the asset unserviceable, delete it, or cancel accountability solely because the three-year ICS renewal becomes due. Only after renewal (and appropriate documentation) mark the prior ICS superseded for document validity; the custodian/accountability continues. Custodian transfers should trigger an updated/new ICS immediately under applicable policy, rather than awaiting the three-year period. Reference: official COA Circular 2022-004 Section 4.1, https://car.dswd.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/COA-CIRCULAR-NO.-2022-004-May-31-2022.pdf.
- **Separate low-value accountability extinguishment:** COA Circular 2022-004 Sections 4.9-4.13 allow ending low-value accountability upon expiry of the independently established *estimated useful life* (or earlier return). That useful life varies by item/agency policy and is **not presumed to be three years**. This is distinct from the three-year ICS renewal. For high-value semi-expendables, expiry of estimated useful life alone does not extinguish liability; return or approved relief is required. Keep a history of both ICS renewal and separate accountability closure. Do not apply COA's low/high semi-expendable rules by default to PPE >= PHP 50,000.
- **Clarified low-value unserviceable policy (owner reconfirmation, 2026-11-02):** An authorized school Supply/Property Officer may **declare a low-value semi-expendable (acquisition cost PHP 5,000 or less) unserviceable without any Division Supply Officer approval step**. The school records condition, inspection basis, reason, reporter, date, custody, and audit trail. For a **high-value semi-expendable (over PHP 5,000 and below PHP 50,000)**, the declaration remains **PENDING_DIVISION_APPROVAL** until an assigned Division Supply/Property Officer approves or rejects it; no premature asset-status change. This is an **internal condition approval** distinction; it is NOT permission to automatically destroy, donate, sell, erase or dispose of low-value property. COA Circular 2022-004 Sections 4.7.10, 4.8–4.11 require IIRUSP and accountability/return treatment for applicable cases. The COA-DBM Joint Circular 2024-1 Revised Manual applies agency Disposal Committee and authorized official approval procedures to disposal. **Do not infer that all high-value disposals require prior COA approval or all low-value disposals are exempt from the Manual solely from the PHP 5,000 classification**; COA's role versus agency disposal approval must be configured based on the applicable 2024 Manual and DepEd-approved delegation/policy, and any special mode-of-disposal requirement. Maintain physical return, formal disposal authorization, signed evidence outside IPDAMS and never hard-delete historical records. PPE above the semi-expendable threshold uses separate rules. Reference official documents: https://car.dswd.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/COA-CIRCULAR-NO.-2022-004-May-31-2022.pdf and https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2024/Joint-Circular/COA-DBM%20JOINT%20CIRCULAR%20NO.%202024-1%20DATED%20JANUARY%2030%2C%202024.pdf. Workflow remains not yet implemented in v0.2.243.
- **Required high-value lifecycle state model (owner clarification, 2026-11-02):** SERVICEABLE → PENDING_UNSERVICEABLE_APPROVAL (no final condition change) → UNSERVICEABLE (first Division approval, **still registered at school**) → PENDING_DISPOSAL_APPROVAL (school prepares separate disposal request and evidence, still registered) → DISPOSAL_APPROVED (second Division approval, still registered pending physical action) → DISPOSED (only on documented completed disposal; removed from *active* school property/accountability, retained in historical archives and audit). Rejection at either step returns to an appropriate non-final state without losing the previous approved facts. Approval audit must record the assigned Division Supply Officer, organization/school scope, reason, timestamp, attachments/references, approver decision, and separation between requester and approver. No shortcut from UNSERVICEABLE to DISPOSED, and no second approval silently inferred from first. If an end-user physically returned the asset before disposal, handle custodian relief separately using evidence; do not assert that unserviceability alone extinguishes employee accountability. PPE and low-value disposal should follow their respective rules and not blindly use this high-value approval workflow.
- **Early breakdown after ICS renewal (owner scenario, 2026-11-02):** ICS renewal establishes continuing accountability for up to the next renewal due date; it does not warrant ongoing operability and does not restrict reporting an event days later. Provide a school-level **Report Defect / Request Inspection** action that immediately records timestamp, observation, asset/custodian, optional inspection evidence, reporter and audit trail; changing condition must not overwrite the ICS issue/renewal history or automatically close accountability. Permit evaluation as repairable (repair lifecycle; preserve ICS while still held by same employee), temporary out-of-service, or confirmed unserviceable. **Only the formal declaration that a high-value semi-expendable item is unserviceable requires Division Supply/Property Officer approval**, per owner policy. A low-value item may be classified unserviceable under school workflow without this extra Division approval, but applicable return and disposal procedures continue. Unserviceable semi-expendable property returned to supply is documented in the RRSP (COA Annex A.6) where relevant and the IIRUSP (COA Annex A.10) for inspection/disposal; high-value end-user accountability is not extinguished merely on a defect report or expiry, and closure requires physical return or authorized relief as applicable. For loss, stolen, damage or destruction use incident report Annex A.9 with separate accountability review; do not conflate mechanical failure with negligence. Never automatically delete/void an item on a repair or unserviceable event. Protect immutability of finalized documents and custody/audit movements.
- Maintain school organization scope, active staff validation, property review, no duplicate asset lines, no stale custodian overwrite, audit history and atomic rollback.
